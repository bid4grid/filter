Microstrip & Discrete Filter Synthesis

Based on a 1990 Diploma Thesis at TH Köln (advisor: Prof. Scheidt), implementing image parameter filter theory and Wheeler / Hammerstad-Jensen microstrip synthesis.

3 · Terminations and topologies

With Ω=f/fc (low-pass) or fc/f (high-pass), the image impedances of a π or T half-section are frequency dependent:

ZIT=Z01−Ω2ZIπ=Z01−Ω2
ZIπm=Z0 1−(1−m2) Ω21−Ω2ZITm=Z0 1−Ω21−(1−m2) Ω2

Rule used by the program: the terminating resistor equals the image impedance at Ω=0.6, where 1−0.62=0.8. The nominal impedance follows from the source resistance:

The topology code is W=10 d1+d2 with d1 for the input end and d2 for the output end (2 = π plain, 3 = π steepened, 4 = T plain, 5 = T steepened; d2=1 for a single half-section). The load that gives zero reflection in the passband is:

  • Symmetric (π→π, T→T): even N∈{2,4,…,10}, odd element count, Ra=Re.
  • Asymmetric (π→T, T→π): odd N∈{1,3,…,9}, even element count; the filter also transforms the impedance. π→T with Re=50 Ω: Z0=40 Ω, Ra=0.64 Re=32 Ω. T→π: Z0=62.5 Ω, Ra=1.5625 Re=78.1 Ω.
Z0 vs Ra: Z0 lives inside the ladder and fixes all L, C and line dimensions; Ra is the external load that terminates port 2 without reflection.

Connecting to a 50 Ω instrument

If Ra≠50 Ω (for example 32 Ω) a direct connection reflects with Γ=50−3250+32≈0.22 (return loss about 13 dB). A quarter-wave microstrip transformer restores the match:

Zt=Ra·50 Ω=32·50=40 Ω,l=λg4=c04fcεeff

A Wilkinson divider behind the filter uses two λ/4 branches of 2·50 Ω≈70.7 Ω and a 100 Ω resistor between the outputs (equal 3 dB split, high isolation).

4 · Band-pass

Lumped ladder

With ωu=2πfu, ωo=2πfo and Δω=ωo−ωu:

series arm (L, C in series):C1=ΔωωuωoZ0,L1=Z0Δω
shunt arm (L, C in parallel):C2=1Z0Δω,L2=Z0Δωωuωo

End elements use C1,L1 (series) and C2,L2 (shunt); interior series arms use C1/2 and 2L1, interior shunt arms 2C2 and L2/2. Every resonator resonates at f0.

Parallel-coupled microstrip lines (Cohn)

n resonators need n+1 coupled sections i=1…n+1. With FBW=(fo−fu)/f0:

g0=π FBW2,gj=2sin (2j−1)π2n
Ji=π FBW/2gi−1 gi(the last section uses g0 again)
Z0e,i=Z0 (1+Ji+Ji2),Z0o,i=Z0 (1−Ji+Ji2)
ρ=Z0eZ0o,si=(0.15+0.42 (ρ−1)) h (0.18…2 mm),li=λg04

The strip width wi comes from the single-line impedance Z0e,iZ0o,i (section 6). The gap formula is an empirical estimate, not a field solution.

Layout. Section k couples resonators Rk−1 and Rk over lk. Section k+1 starts where section k ends, so Rk runs through two sections (about λ/2) and each resonator sits one strip width plus one gap lower than the previous one: the edge-coupled staircase. R0 and Rn+1 are the input and output lines. Screen, DXF and KiCad use the same coordinates.

5 · Microstrip realization

Line model (Hammerstad & Jensen)

u=wh,εeff=εr+12+εr−12 f(u)
u<1:f=11+12/u+0.04 (1−u)2,Z=60εeffln 8u+u4
u≥1:f=11+12/u,Z=120πεeff (u+1.393+0.667ln (u+1.444))

Synthesis (impedance → width) inverts this model by bisection over 0.02≤u≤40; outside that range the limit is used and a warning appears. The model is static, lossless and ignores dispersion and conductor thickness.

Stepped-impedance low-pass / high-pass

Inductors become narrow lines (ZH), capacitors wide lines (ZL); both impedances are adjustable (default 120 Ω / 20 Ω). Lengths include the correction for the line capacitance and inductance:

lL=ωcL λgH2πZH(1−(Z0/ZH)2)
lC=ZL ωcC λgL2π(1−(ZL/Z0)2)

For an end inductor (ωcL=Z0) the electrical length is θ=Z0/ZH1−(Z0/ZH)2, independent of fc – physical lengths scale with the wavelength, electrical lengths do not. Interior elements are twice as large, so their lines are about twice as long. Beyond λ/8 the lumped approximation fails; the Microstrip overlay in the response plot shows the effect.

Open-end fringing

Δl=0.412 h εeff+0.3εeff−0.258·u+0.264u+0.8

With Compensate open-end fringing on, every open end is shortened by Δl: each wide stepped-impedance line once (never below 25 % of its length) and every open resonator end of a coupled-line filter.
